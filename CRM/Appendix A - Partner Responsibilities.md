## Appendix A \- USAi Partner Agency Responsibilities

This matrix is designed for federal agencies preparing to integrate USAi services. It provides clear delineation of responsibilities between your agency, GSA, and AI service providers. Whether you're a CISO planning security implementations, a program manager overseeing AI adoption, or a compliance officer ensuring regulatory adherence, this document contains the structured guidance you need.

The framework emphasizes a collaborative approach while ensuring each party understands their specific obligations for maintaining security, managing risk, and delivering mission value through responsible AI implementation.

**Updates to the Responsibilities:**  
This responsibility matrix will continue to evolve based on updated federal guidance, USAi capabilities, emerging security standards, industry best practices, and feedback from partner agencies and commercial providers. As the AI landscape advances and new threats emerge, GSA will regularly update these requirements in collaboration with federal stakeholders to ensure USAi services remain secure, effective, and aligned with government-wide AI policies and risk management frameworks.

**What You'll Find Inside:**

* **Security & Privacy Responsibilities (Table 1):** Detailed implementation guidance across 12 critical security domains, from identity management and encryption to incident response and risk authorization.  
* **AI Lifecycle Management (Table 2):** A unified responsibility model covering everything from procurement and onboarding through operations and governance, with special provisions for high-impact AI use cases.

Note that separate rows are outlined for High-Risk AI use cases and only apply when an agency determines a use case is “high-impact” as defined in M-25-21, where the AI's output is a principal basis for decisions affecting rights or safety.¹

| **Lifecycle Phase: Procurement & Onboarding** |  |  |  |  |
| Supplier & Model Vetting |  | • Process Federal information only in FedRAMP-authorized cloud environments or demonstrate equivalent controls.² • Participate in phased FedRAMP authorization processes for non FedRAMP-authorized services handling or impacting federal information. • Provide documentation of training data sources and usage rights, and grant the government sufficient data rights to enable portability.³ • Maximize use of US-developed AI and justify any non-US components.⁴ • Maintain current security documentation and attestations. | • Review the security posture and authorization status of services. • Clearly denote any non-FedRAMP authorized cloud used to handle federal information and associated security review and authorization pathway. • Perform SCRM review on model and service providers. • Maintain an approved supplier list with model cards, SCRM artifacts, and contractual language for data rights & IP. | • Review GSA's security assessments and risk documentation for alignment with agency requirements. • Set agency guardrails for use of any non-FedRAMP authorized services (e.g., data type restrictions). • Review GSA's SCRM artifacts. • Obtain formal approval from internal governance.
